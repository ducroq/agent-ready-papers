# Whetten 1989

## Bibliographic Info
- **Authors:** David A. Whetten
- **Year:** 1989
- **Title:** What Constitutes a Theoretical Contribution?
- **Venue:** Academy of Management Review, 14(4), 490-495
- **DOI:** 10.5465/amr.1989.4308371
- **Status:** READ (full text, 2026-10-16)

## Summary
Identifies the essential building blocks of a theoretical contribution: What (factors/constructs), How (relationships), Why (underlying logic/warrants), and Who/Where/When (boundary conditions). A theoretical contribution must change our understanding of at least one of these.

## Key Findings
- Four building blocks: What, How, Why, Who/Where/When
- "Why" (the logical reasoning connecting constructs to outcomes) is the most critical and most often missing
- Theory is the EXPLANATION -- not references, data, variables, diagrams, or hypotheses (per Sutton & Staw 1995)
- Boundary conditions (where the theory applies) are essential but often unstated

## Verified quotes (full text read 2026-10-16; PDF `literature/pdfs/whetten-1989.pdf`, gitignored; all 16 FOUND by `tools/check_quotes`; printed page = 489 + PDF page)

- Elements: "a complete theory must contain four essential elements" (p. 490, after Dubin 1978): What, How, Why, Who/Where/When. "Together the What and How elements constitute the domain or subject of the theory" (p. 491).
- Why: "This rationale constitutes the theory's assumptions", "the theoretical glue that welds the model together"; "What and How describe; only Why explains"; "During the theory-development process, logic replaces data as the basis for evaluation" (p. 491).
- Propositions: "propositions involve concepts, whereas hypotheses require measures" (p. 491); "If propositions are used, they should be limited to specifying the logically deduced implications for research of a theoretical argument"; "Some authors mistakenly use propositions to summarize a body of literature"; "Not all bona fide theoretical contributions require propositions" (p. 492).
- Who/Where/When: "These conditions place limitations on the propositions generated from a theoretical model"; "These temporal and contextual factors set the boundaries of generalizability, and as such constitute the range of the theory" (p. 492).
- Evidence for a critique: "This evidence can be logical (e.g., the theory is not internally consistent), empirical (its predictions are inconsistent with the data accumulated from several studies), or epistemological (its assumptions are invalid" (p. 493).
- Seven reviewer questions answering "What constitutes a publishable theory paper?" (p. 494): What's new? So what? Why so? Well done? Done well? Why now? Who cares? E.g. "Does the paper make a significant, value-added contribution to current thinking?"; "Are the underlying logic and supporting evidence compelling?"

**Correction to the paraphrase above (2026-10-16):** a Whetten *proposition* is a logically deduced, researchable relationship between concepts, not a recommendation; the framework's PROPOSITION type (DR-004) borrowed the word with a different meaning. Whetten's four elements and seven questions judge a *contribution* (a theory paper), not a single statement. Both points feed DR-027.

## Relevance to DR-004
The "Why" component maps directly to Toulmin's warrant. A theoretical paper's core contribution is not a claim that can be source-checked -- it's a logical argument (warrant) connecting constructs. Verification must shift from "does a source say this?" to "is the reasoning valid and are the premises true?" The What/How/Why/When framework provides a natural structure for a "proposition registry" as an alternative to the claim registry.
