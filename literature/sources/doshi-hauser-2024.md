# Doshi & Hauser 2024 — AI ideas raise individual story ratings, lower collective diversity (L83)

**Reading status:** READ (main text, Europe PMC open-access copy PMC11244532, 2026-09-27; Supplementary Tables S1–S18 not read). Step 0 PASS (Crossref).

## Bibliographic Info
- **Authors:** Anil R. Doshi, Oliver P. Hauser
- **Year:** 2024
- **Title:** Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content
- **Venue:** Science Advances 10(28): eadn5290 (CC BY 4.0)
- **DOI:** [10.1126/sciadv.adn5290](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290)

## Key Findings
- Preregistered. 293 lay writers (Prolific, UK) wrote 8-sentence stories: human only, one GPT-4 premise, or up to five. 600 blind evaluators, 3,519 ratings on 9-point scales.
- Five ideas: novelty +8.1% (b = 0.311), usefulness +9.0% (b = 0.453), more enjoyable (b = 0.375), **less boring** (b = −0.200, P = 0.049, marginal), better written.
- Gains accrue to less creative writers (enjoyment up to +22.6%). High-creativity writers show little effect.
- **Homogenisation:** AI-assisted stories are more similar to each other (embedding cosine; b = 0.871 with one idea, b = 0.718 with five). "writers are individually better off, but collectively a narrower scope of novel content is produced."

## Caveats
- The AI supplied premises, not prose. Short texts, lay writers, no iteration. Similarity was computed, not judged by readers.
- Tier **A**.

## Relevance
- Human prose on AI premises was rated higher by lay readers. It says nothing about AI-written prose.
- The cost is sameness *between* authors, which neither a per-text nor a single-author scan can see (DR-022 point 4).
