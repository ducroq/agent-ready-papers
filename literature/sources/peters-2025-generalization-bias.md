## Summary
Ten LLMs (among them ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT-4.5, DeepSeek, LLaMA 3.3 70B and Claude 3.7 Sonnet) summarised abstracts and articles from top science and medical journals: 4,900 summaries, compared with the originals and with expert summaries from NEJM Journal Watch.

## Key Findings
- Abstract: "Even when explicitly prompted for accuracy, most LLMs produced broader generalizations of scientific results than those in the original texts, with DeepSeek, ChatGPT-4o, and LLaMA 3.3 70B overgeneralizing in 26–73% of cases."
- Abstract: compared with human-written summaries, "LLM summaries were nearly five times more likely to contain broad generalizations (odds ratio = 4.85, 95% CI [3.06, 7.70])"; "newer models tended to perform worse".
- Coding scheme: a *generic generalization* is a present-tense statement with no quantifier that describes results "as if they apply to whole categories".

## Caveats (tier discipline)
- These are summaries, not citations in research papers; one time point; the models were tested in 2024–25.
