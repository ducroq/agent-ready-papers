## What to extract (→ claim map)
- The three layers as the authors define them: **(1)** technical — reliability, robustness, explainability; **(2)** governance and regulation — translating ethical principles into enforceable rules; **(3)** human-AI interaction — trust formation, calibration, anthropomorphism
- The stated interdependence claim — that trustworthiness requires satisfaction across all three simultaneously. Two different quotations circulate in aggregator coverage; neither is verified, so quote nothing from this paper until the text is in hand
- Method: reportedly 43 CSSCI-indexed Chinese-language articles plus four policy reports — unverified
- The distinctive contribution claimed for Chinese scholarship: whether trust is the right concept for AI at all, and a proposal that AI can function as a **trustee**

## Relevance to the framework
This paper is the **trigger** for the 2026-09-14 field scan, not a source for it. Its relation to this repo is a contrast worth stating precisely, because the vocabularies overlap and the questions do not:

- Their unit of analysis is a **system property** — is this AI system trustworthy?
- This framework's unit is a **document property** — is this text's confidence calibrated to its evidence?

The framework's stated position (`docs/framework-summary.md`, grounding note) is that it sits *downstream* of model-layer trustworthiness: a well-grounded, trustworthy model can still produce un-warranted arguments and mis-calibrated confidence. That is close to a claim that Layer 1 is insufficient in principle — a mildly adversarial stance toward the three-layer project, and one worth making explicit if this is ever cited.

Their Layer 2 has a real analogue here at a much smaller scale: the Hard Constraints block in `CLAUDE.md` is project-scoped governance with an override property, and it already documents its own limits (see [L56](palmblad-2026.md)).
