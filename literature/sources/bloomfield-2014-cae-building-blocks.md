## Summary
Introduces five CAE "building blocks" (decomposition, substitution, evidence incorporation and others) derived from a review of real assurance cases in the defence, medical, financial and nuclear sectors. Each block pairs an argument with a "side-warrant" stating why the claim follows from the sub-claims. The paper explicitly grounds CAE arguments in Toulmin's warrant and backing (ref. [11], Toulmin 1958, our L01).

## Key Findings
- Claims are "Claims – assertions put forward for general acceptance"; "Claims that are asserted as true without justification become assumptions" (PDF p. 2).
- Toulmin as the source: "Arguments link the evidence to the claim. They are defined as Toulmin’s warrants" (PDF p. 2); "As discussed above, an argument is initially defined as Toulmin’s warrant" and "in Toulmin’s terminology backing may be introduced to justify the general validity of the warrant" (PDF p. 3).
- Proportionality: "the more onerous the claims are for a system, the more precision and rigour is needed in how they are justified" (PDF p. 3).
- "Narrative is an essential part of the case" (PDF p. 3), and in practice "the practice of how to structure and present cases is very varied" (PDF p. 3).

## Relevance to agent-ready-papers
The only source of the three that states the Toulmin lineage outright, so Paper 1 draft 2 could use it to link its Toulmin-based framing (L01) to the assurance-case tradition rather than claim the combination as new. Its proportionality principle (more onerous claims need more rigour) may parallel DR-002's tier-to-language mapping, and its point that narrative remains essential could support keeping prose primary with the registry as a companion. It is a workshop paper read in its accepted version, so page references should be checked against the IEEE version before citing.
