## Summary
Sets "a common approach and calibrated language" for expressing the degree of certainty in key findings across the three IPCC working groups. Two metrics: **confidence**, "in the validity of a finding, based on the type, amount, quality, and consistency of evidence (e.g., mechanistic understanding, theory, data, models, expert judgment) and the degree of agreement", expressed qualitatively; and **likelihood**, a quantified probability.

## Key Findings
- Evidence is summarised as "limited", "medium" or "robust"; agreement as "low", "medium" or "high". Confidence is judged from both, on five levels: "very low", "low", "medium", "high", "very high".
- Likelihood terms are calibrated to probability ranges: "Virtually certain 99-100%", "Very likely 90-100%", "Likely 66-100%", "About as likely as not 33 to 66%", "Unlikely 0-33%", "Very unlikely 0-10%", "Exceptionally unlikely 0-1%".
- Authors must "provide a traceable account describing your evaluation of evidence and agreement".
- Warns that a group tends "to converge on an expressed view and become overconfident in it".
- Confidence "is distinct from 'statistical confidence'" and is not to be read probabilistically.
