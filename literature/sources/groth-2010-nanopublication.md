## Summary
Defines a nanopublication as a core scientific statement with its context (annotations, provenance, attribution), serialised as Named Graphs in RDF, and argues for aggregating the nanopublications about one statement.

## Key Findings
- Abstract (p. 51): nanopublications are "core scientific statements with associated context", promoted because statements repeated across fora make it "difficult to determine attribution, quality and provenance".
- Checking is left to others (p. 54): a nanopublication as a whole can be attributed, approved by a reviewer, voted on or cited; signatures "may be important in verifying claims"; users "may decide which accounts they trust and which they do not based on any number of heuristics".
- S-Evidence (p. 55): all nanopublications about the same statement; aggregators "find, filter and combine all the evidence for a statement ... to ascertain the veracity of a statement".

## Relevance to agent-ready-papers
Supports Paper 1's S4-7: nanopublications record a statement with its provenance and define no procedure for checking a step; verification there means attribution, signatures and pooling evidence across statements.
