# Glossary

**Paper:** The Verification Gap (Paper 1)
**Domains:** argumentation theory, scholarly communication, research reporting, systems engineering

## Framework terms

This paper uses the framework's terms as defined in [`docs/glossary.md`](../../docs/glossary.md): claim, grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier, rebuttal, confidence tier, source check, warrant check, declared starting point, contribution, verification, validation, priority. They are not repeated here. Below are the paper's own terms and the framework terms it uses in a narrower sense.

> Status: written 2026-10-16 for draft 2, under DR-027 and DR-028 (both Proposed). The framework glossary is a draft until DR-027 is accepted. Registry IDs D2–D5 and C1 refer to the DR-027 trial migration of this paper's registry; they enter the tracked registry when it migrates. Definitions here are the draft-2 wording; the registry's D rows follow them on migration.

## Paper terms

| Term | Definition in this paper | Registry | Notes |
|------|--------------------------|----------|-------|
| **claim register** | The table, published with the paper, that records each claim with its grounds, check and confidence tier, and the contribution with its check. Toulmin's layout gives the claim rows; Whetten's account of a contribution gives the contribution row. | — | Defined in Box 1 from draft 3 (2026-10-19), after the maintainer's feedback that the paper never said what a register is. |
| **generative AI tools** | Software that drafts or revises text with a large language model; "AI" in the paper means these. | — | Feedback #4: "AI" is defined once, then used. |
| **AI-augmented writing** | Drafting and revision assisted by general-purpose large language models. | D4 | |
| **non-empirical paper** | A paper whose main contribution is reasoning rather than new data: theoretical, conceptual, design-science and perspective papers are examples, not a closed list. | D3 (own) | The reviewers of draft 1 rejected the four types as a taxonomy (#10); draft 2 uses them as examples. |
| **verification infrastructure** | Reporting requirements plus checks that authors and reviewers apply before and during peer review, as EQUATOR's guidelines do for empirical health research. | D2 | |
| **detection vs verification** | Detection asks who wrote a text; verification asks whether it holds. | D5 | |
| **reporting guideline** | A checklist of items a manuscript of a given study type should report (e.g. CONSORT, PRISMA). | — | Distinct from a quality-appraisal scale such as SANRA, which its authors call "not a reporting guideline". |
| **confidence inflation** | Wording that expresses more certainty than the evidence supports. | S1-2 | The paper's own term, labelled as such. |

## Terms used in a narrower sense

| Term | Framework meaning | This paper's use |
|------|-------------------|------------------|
| **contribution** | Each contribution a paper makes (root claims). | One: C1 (registry), the call for verification infrastructure for non-empirical papers. |
| **validation** | Checking that a contribution serves its audience and purpose. | Only the contribution check; the paper reports no user study. |
