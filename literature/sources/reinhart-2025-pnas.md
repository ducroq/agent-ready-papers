## Key Findings
- Human texts continued by six models (GPT-4o, GPT-4o Mini, Llama 3 8B/70B base and instruct). 66 Biber features.
- A 7-way classifier reaches 66% accuracy (chance 14%); only 4.2% of LLM texts were misread as human.
- GPT-4o uses present participial clauses at 5.3 times the human rate; the instruction-tuned models use nominalisations at 1.5 to 2 times the human rate. This is a dense, noun-heavy style.
- Base models are nearer human rates: "instruction tuning appears" to drive the difference (the authors hedge throughout), and size does not help.
- Names "a preference for grandiose, if hollow, summative sentences".

## Caveats
- Continuation task, not free composition. No limitations section. Hedging results, if any, are in the unread SI.
- Tier **A**.

## Relevance
- Register-level evidence, hedged by the authors, that the house style comes from tuning. It measures distance from human style, not similarity between texts. Names the tidy-conclusion cue.
- Nominalisation is a candidate academic-prose signal (compare Sword's concrete nouns, L90).
