## Summary
A book-length study of hedging in research articles, combining a corpus analysis of journal articles with interviews with scientists, to explain hedges pragmatically as part of how claims gain acceptance in a discipline. Chapters (publisher ToC) cover surface features of hedging (pp. 102-155), a pragmatic analysis (pp. 156-188), hedges in scientific discourse (pp. 189-216) and second-language writers (pp. 217-243).

## Key Findings
From the publisher's description only (not the book text):
- Hedging is tied to community practice: "the extensive use of possibility and tentativeness in research writing is intimately connected to the social and institutional practices of academic communities".
- Hedging "is at the heart of how knowledge comes to be socially accredited through texts".
- "The results show that hedging is central to Scientific argument".
