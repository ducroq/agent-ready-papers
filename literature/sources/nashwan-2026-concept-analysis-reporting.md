## Summary
First stage of an EQUATOR-guided reporting guideline for concept analysis, a non-empirical paper type in nursing. The authors review 111 published concept analyses against a data-extraction tool that grew to 66 items under eight headings, report how often each item was reported, and plan a Delphi study to turn the items into a checklist. Discrepancy to note: the abstract names MEDLINE, CINAHL and PsycINFO, the Methods name PubMed, Scopus and CINAHL.

## Key Findings
- The gap (Introduction): "No reporting guidelines for concept analysis in nursing research are listed on the EQUATOR network".
- Item development (Methods, Data collection process): items were added after piloting, and "The final version of the data extraction tool contained 66 items".
- Structure (Results): "These items are categorised under eight key subheadings" (Title and Abstract, Background, Methods, Ethics, Results, Discussion, Limitations, Conclusion).
- Where reporting fails (Results): "major weaknesses were evident in the Methods, particularly in the Search, Data and Analysis components".
- A cause relevant to any non-empirical genre (Bridging the reporting gap): "concept analysis is frequently perceived as purely theoretical, resulting in underreporting of methodological details".
- Limitation: "a formal quality appraisal could not be conducted, as no validated tool currently exists for evaluating concept analysis studies in nursing".

## Relevance to agent-ready-papers
A recent example of building reporting items for a paper type that has no data, which is Paper 1's position. Draft 2 could cite it to suggest that the absence of reporting standards for non-empirical papers is recognised outside our field, and that its "purely theoretical, therefore under-reported methods" pattern may also apply to perspective papers. It is a review stage, not a finished guideline: the checklist awaits a Delphi round, so it supports "a need is recognised" rather than "a standard exists".
