## Summary
Reviews the methods of studies of quotation accuracy in medical journals. 15 studies met the criteria for the primary quantitative analysis, and their error rates were recalculated with consistent criteria.

## Key Findings
- Abstract: the recalculated quotation error rate is 14.5% (95% CI 10.5–18.6%). Of the content errors, 64.8% are major ("the referenced source either fails to substantiate, is unrelated to, or contradicts the assertion"), and 35.2% are minor, which "are an oversimplification, overgeneralization, or trivial inaccuracies".
- p. 6: "a minor error was an oversimplification, a generalization, or a trivial inaccuracy that did not change the intended meaning of the original authors". In a Table 2 example (p. 7), a source found no association between serum retinol and *breast* cancer, and was cited for no association with "cancer".

## Caveats (tier discipline)
- Medical journals only, English only, and studies are heterogeneous. Generalisation is not counted separately from oversimplification and trivial inaccuracy, so there is no rate for overgeneralisation alone.
- Relays the primary quotation-accuracy studies (count as one origin with them).
