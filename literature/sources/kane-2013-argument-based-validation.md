## Summary
Sets out the argument-based approach to validation: the claims drawn from test scores are laid out as an interpretation/use argument (IUA), and validation evaluates that argument. Kane offers Toulmin's model as the framework for the IUA's presumptive inferences (pp. 11–12) and applies it explicitly to the scoring inference (p. 25: "Using Toulmin's model, the performances are the data and the score is the claim"); he does not say every inference is laid out that way.

## Key Findings
- Abstract (p. 1): "the claims based on the test scores be outlined as an argument that specifies the inferences and supporting assumptions needed to get from test responses to score-based interpretations and uses. Validation then can be thought of as an evaluation of the coherence and completeness of this interpretation/use argument and of the plausibility of its inferences and assumptions."
- A procedure (p. 9): "It is clear where to begin (by specifying the IUA), how to proceed (by evaluating the coherence and completeness of the IUA and the plausibility of its inferences and assumptions), and when to stop (when the inferences and assumptions have been evaluated)."
- Toulmin as the framework (pp. 11–12): Figure 1, "Toulmin's Model of Inference"; "each inference starts from a datum and makes a claim. The inference relies on a warrant"; warrants need backing; qualifiers may be words ("sometimes," "usually," or "almost always") or quantitative; Toulmin's exceptions, or conditions of rebuttal, mark where a warrant does not apply.
- Inferences in IUAs are presumptive, as in "science, academic discourse, and practical reasoning" (p. 11).
