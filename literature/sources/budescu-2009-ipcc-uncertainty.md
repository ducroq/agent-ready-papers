# Budescu, Broomell & Por 2009 — Communicating uncertainty in IPCC reports (L112)

## Bibliographic Info
- **Authors:** David V. Budescu, Stephen Broomell, Han-Hui Por (University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign)
- **Year:** 2009
- **Title:** Improving Communication of Uncertainty in the Reports of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change
- **Venue:** Psychological Science 20(3), 299–308. DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02284.x (Crossref, 2026-10-18)
- **File:** `pdfs/budescu-2009-ipcc-uncertainty.pdf` (gitignored; supplied by the maintainer). PDF p. 1 = journal p. 299.
- **Status:** READ 2026-10-19: abstract, method, results (control and translation groups), summary and recommendations

## Summary
An experiment in which subjects read sentences from the 2007 IPCC report and gave numerical values for its probability terms. Judgements deviated from the IPCC guidelines even for subjects shown the guidelines; adding numerical ranges to the terms in the text improved consistency.

## Key Findings
- Abstract (p. 299): "The respondents' judgments deviated significantly from the IPCC guidelines, even when the respondents had access to these guidelines. These results suggest that the method used by the IPCC is likely to convey levels of imprecision that are too high."
- Design (pp. 300–301): control group (terms only), translation group (shown the IPCC guidelines), and two groups with numerical ranges beside each term (wide, narrow).
- Example (Summary): 25% of subjects read "very likely" as below 70%.
- Recommendation: use both verbal terms and numerical values.
