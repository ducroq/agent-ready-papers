## Summary
Quasi-experiment with experimental and control groups of Chinese university EFL students; the experimental group received Toulmin-model scaffolding. Essays were coded for Toulmin elements (claim, qualifier, reason, evidence, warrant, counterargument, rebuttal) and each element's quality was scored; overall scores and element number/type/quality were compared with mixed-effects models.

## Key Findings
- Elements are scored for quality, not only counted: "The authors scored the quality of argumentative elements according to McCann (1989)" (§4.3).
- The warrant is judged by the inference it licenses: "The quality of warrants depends on the logical relationship between reasons + evidence and claims" (§4.3).
- Two markers; ICC 0.94 for overall score (§4.3).
- Toulmin scaffolding had a significant effect on most argumentative elements.

## Relevance to agent-ready-papers
Second peer-reviewed instance of Toulmin's elements used as scoring criteria for argument quality in university writing (and the rubric source for L106). Its warrant criterion (does the warrant connect reasons and evidence to the claim?) is close to the check S3-1 proposes.

## Caveats
- Student EFL essays in a teaching intervention, not research papers.
- Quality criteria come from McCann (1989), adapted to Toulmin's dimensions; the criteria are not the author's own validation.
