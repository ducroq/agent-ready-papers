# Porter & Machery 2024 — Lay readers cannot tell AI poems apart, and prefer them (L85)

## Bibliographic Info
- **Authors:** Brian Porter, Edouard Machery
- **Year:** 2024
- **Title:** AI-generated poetry is indistinguishable from human-written poetry and is rated more favorably
- **Venue:** Scientific Reports 14: 26133
- **DOI:** [10.1038/s41598-024-76900-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-76900-1)
- **Local copy:** `../pdfs/porter-machery-2024.pdf`

## Key Findings
- Preregistered. 50 poems by 10 canonical poets and 50 ChatGPT-3.5 imitations (first five generated, unselected).
- Study 1 (N = 1,634): discrimination below chance, 46.6%.
- Study 2 (N = 696): AI poems rated higher on overall quality (d = 0.671) and most on rhythm (d = 0.847). They are higher on 13 of 14 dimensions (9 significant in the mixed model) but **not on "original"** (d = 0.040). Being *told* a poem is AI lowers ratings.
- Mechanism offered: "the more easily-understood AI-generated poems are on average preferred by these readers". 89% of the AI poems rhyme.

## Caveats
- Non-expert readers, difficult canonical poems against accessible imitations, and a single model generation.
- Tier **A** for lay-reader preference; narrow stimuli.

## Relevance
- **"Boring" depends on the audience.** Lay readers reward accessibility; the one dimension where AI gains nothing is originality. A readability lens must name its reader.
