## Summary
A review of empirical studies of automation complacency and automation bias in human interaction with automated and decision-support systems (aviation, process control, medicine). It argues that the two are overlapping phenomena driven by attention, and proposes an integrated model.

## Key Findings
- Abstract (p. 381): "Automation bias results in making both omission and commission errors when decision aids are imperfect. Automation bias occurs in both naive and expert participants, cannot be prevented by training or instructions, and can affect decision making in individuals as well as in teams."
- p. 391: an error of omission is one "whereby the user does not respond to a critical situation" that the aid failed to flag; in a commission error, "users follow the advice of the aid even though it is incorrect."
- p. 397: automation bias "cannot be prevented by training or explicit instructions to verify the recommendations of an aid".

## Caveats (tier discipline)
- Predates LLMs: the studies concern alarms, flight-deck aids, process control and medical decision support. Applying it to AI-drafted text is an inference.
- A review: the findings are the authors' synthesis of other studies, so a citation should say "in the studies they reviewed".
