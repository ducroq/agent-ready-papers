# Publisher and standards-body policy on AI text and detectors, 2026 (L77–L80)

**Reading status:** READ (the operative sections, accessed 2026-09-27). URLs resolve unless noted. Policy pages are primary sources for *what the policy says*, not for whether detectors work.

| ID | Source | Operative text | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| L77 | ICMJE Recommendations, updated January 2026, §V "Use of Artificial Intelligence in Publishing" ([authors page](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html)) | V.A: journals should require authors to disclose AI use at submission and describe how it was used; chatbots cannot be authors. "Nondisclosure of AI use may require corrective action and may be construed as misconduct in some circumstances" (re-checked live 2026-09-27). V.C: editors should query AI use. **No mention of detectors.** | B |

| L79 | Springer Nature: press release of 2025-04-30 on donating its research-integrity AI tool, and the Nature Portfolio editorial policy "Artificial Intelligence (AI)" | The tool scores manuscript sections for the probability of AI-generated text across its journals and books; "The higher the score, the greater the probability of there being problems, initiating a check by human assessors". It is donated to the STM Integrity Hub. The policy tiers AI use: extensive writing support is "Permitted with human oversight, verification, and transparency through disclosure" (re-checked live 2026-09-27) | D |
| L80 | Elsevier, "Generative AI policies for journals", updated June 2026 | Disclose AI use in a separate AI declaration, which is published with the article; basic grammar and spelling checks need none. Mentions "research integrity checks" but names no AI-text detector | D |

**Grey context, not indexed separately:**
- OpenAI withdrew its AI classifier on 2023-07-20 "due to its low rate of accuracy" (26% TPR, 9% FPR). Read via the Wayback snapshot of 2023-08-01.
- Vanderbilt University disabled Turnitin's AI detector in August 2023: at a claimed 1% FPR, "around 750 student papers could have been incorrectly labeled" a year.

**Not covered:** Science/AAAS (pages blocked), Wiley, Taylor & Francis, IEEE. CANGARU is only a protocol (arXiv:2307.08974). TRIPOD-LLM covers studies that *use* LLMs, not AI-written manuscripts.

**What follows:** every policy read judges compliance on disclosure and human accountability. Where a publisher scores text (Springer Nature), a flag leads to human review. How reviewers weigh a flag against a disclosure is not documented, and the Springer Nature tool's error rates are not published.
