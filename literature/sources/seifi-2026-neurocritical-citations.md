## Summary
GPT-5.3, DeepSeek-V3 and Grok-4 were queried on 10 March 2026 through their paid web interfaces, retrieval and browsing disabled, zero-shot, with an explicit instruction to avoid fabrication. Each gave 10 references (2020 or later) for each of 10 neurocritical care topics: 300 references. Two blinded expert raters scored each against PubMed, doi.org, Google Scholar and Crossref on a 0–3 scale (3 = nonexistent).

## Key Findings
- Results, *Overall*: "Of 300 references evaluated, 165 (55.0%) contained a verifiable citation inaccuracy (hallucination rate) and 85 (28.3%) corresponded to no existing publication (fabrication rate)." Inter-rater κ = 0.91.
- Results, *Model-Level Performance*: fabrication 8% DeepSeek-V3, 27% GPT-5.3, 50% Grok-4 (100 references each).
- Discussion: "Many fabricated references were structurally complete, bearing plausible author names, recognizable journal titles, and coherent volume and page designations, and were indistinguishable from legitimate citations, without deliberate cross-database verification." (an observation, not a measured outcome)
- Identifier analysis: 3 fabricated references (3.5%) carried a correctly resolving identifier ("false reassurance").

## Caveats (tier discipline)
- Retrieval disabled by design: the rates are for recall from training data, not for search-enabled or deep-research modes; the authors cite a RAG study with no hallucinations.
- One time point, one sample per model-topic cell, topics chosen by one expert; topic-level results exploratory.
