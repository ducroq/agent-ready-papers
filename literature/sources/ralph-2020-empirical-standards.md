## Key Findings
- The standards are "a type of evidence standard … like the CONSORT and PRISMA statements", meant to "both reflect, and help build, consensus" (README).
- One standard is "Engineering Research (AKA Design Science)", with an Essential checklist for manuscripts "that propose and evaluate technological artifacts".
- "Empirical" is defined as research "that uses data rather than a mathematical proof or a philosophical treatise".
- The "Methodological Guidelines and Meta-Science" standard covers papers that "may or may not include primary or secondary empirical data" and "may consider philosophical or practical issues". It requires "clear, valid arguments supporting recommendations" and lists "informal logical fallacies" as antipatterns. The Engineering Research standard asks for "correctness arguments for the key analytical and theoretical contributions".

## Relevance
Paper 1 S2-2: the closest existing instruments, named in the text. They ask for valid arguments but give no procedure for verifying them; that is the narrower gap Paper 1 can claim.

## Caveats
- Community-maintained, not produced by a formal consensus method (Delphi); a reviewer may still call it consensus-based, which is why the paper names it.
- Tier **D** (community standard; the arXiv paper is not peer-reviewed).
