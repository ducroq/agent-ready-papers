## Summary
Quasi-experimental mixed-methods study of 40 first-year ESL engineering students who wrote argumentative essays before and after using DeepL Write and Claude. Essay quality is scored with a rubric built on Toulmin's elements; pre/post differences are tested with t-tests and ANOVA per component.

## Key Findings
- The scoring instrument is Toulmin-based: "The Toulmin model of argumentation-based rubric was used to analyse the argumentative essays" (p. 1).
- Elements are scored, not just identified: "It evaluated five elements in the Toulmin argumentation model: claim, grounds, warrant, backing, and rebuttal" and "Each element was scored on a scale of 0 to 3, giving a total score of 15" (p. 5).
- Two trained raters; inter-rater ICC(3) = 0.87 (p. 5). Rubric adapted from Yang 2022 (L107).
- Qualifier is not scored (five of the six elements).

## Relevance to agent-ready-papers
Supports S3-1's narrower proposition that Toulmin's elements can be operationalised as criteria for *assessing* argument quality in student writing, with acceptable rater agreement. It does not show verification of academic papers: the genre is student argumentative essays, the purpose is measuring learning gains, and quality of each element is judged by human raters on a rubric.

## Caveats
- Student ESL essays, not research papers; the transfer to academic manuscripts is this repo's extension.
- The paper's own focus is AI-tool effectiveness; the rubric is instrument, not object of study, and is not separately validated beyond ICC.
