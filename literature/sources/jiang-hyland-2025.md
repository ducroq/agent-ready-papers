## Key Findings
- 145 ChatGPT-4 essays (one persuasive prompt pattern) against 145 British second-year student essays (LOCNESS). Hyland's five engagement categories; 97% coder agreement.
- Engagement per 1,000 words: ChatGPT 5.40 vs students 16.99 (LL = 471.98, p < 0.001; p. 12).
- Table 2 (p. 14): reader mentions 2.20 vs 6.39; directives 1.84 vs 6.12; questions 0.08 vs 1.72; personal asides 0.00 vs 1.17. Appeals to shared knowledge make up a larger share for ChatGPT (23.66% vs 9.35%).
- ChatGPT shows "much narrower standard deviations and smaller dispersion" (p. 26). The SDs scale with the lower mean (coefficient of variation 0.057 vs 0.052), but the dispersion of proportions is also lower (DP 0.22 vs 0.46, Table 2), which does indicate more even use across essays. Caveat: the essays were generated in batches ("Write 16 argumentative essays …").

## Caveats
- Student essays, not research articles; one model and one prompt pattern. The authors: more interactional devices "do not necessarily mean more effective texts".
- Tier **B**.

## Relevance
- **A countable academic-prose signal the scanner lacks:** engagement-marker density, with personal asides and questions nearly absent in LLM text. No research-article reference rate.
- Engagement, not stance: nothing on hedges or boosters.
