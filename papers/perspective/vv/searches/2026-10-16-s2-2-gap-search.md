# Gap search for claim S2-2 (2026-10-16)

**Claim tested (S2-2, draft 2):** "Guidance for papers without new data exists but is scattered; no consensus checklist was found for verifying the reasoning of non-empirical papers (whether each step from evidence to claim holds)."

**Ground type:** `own:` (the author's own search). It should be judged by how rigorous the method is (DR-027, DR-008), not by who is cited.
**Run by:** an AI agent (Claude Code) for the author, 2026-10-16, through anonymous endpoints only. No email, mailto or API key was sent.
**Raw data and scripts:** `2026-10-16-s2-2-data/` next to this file holds the query files, the JSON results of every database query (top 30 records each), the full EQUATOR title list and the scripts that produced them. Re-running `python3 -I dbsearch.py queries-round1.tsv out.json` (and round 2) repeats the database step. Counts will drift as the indexes grow.

## 1. Purpose

S2-2 is a claim that something does not exist, so it can only be as strong as the search behind it. This search tries to falsify it: it looks for one counterexample, meaning a consensus checklist that verifies the reasoning of non-empirical papers. It records the search so that a reader can repeat it.

## 2. Inclusion criteria (fixed before searching)

A **counterexample** must meet all three criteria:

- **(a) Consensus or community standard.** A reporting guideline or checklist developed by a formal consensus method (Delphi, consensus meeting) or adopted as a community standard (for example, issued by a scholarly society or methods body). Proposals by a single author or editor do not qualify.
- **(b) Papers without new data.** It applies to theoretical, conceptual, perspective, position or opinion papers, or to reviews of arguments. A guideline for empirical studies that has one item on theory or discussion does not qualify.
- **(c) Reasoning items.** It contains items for verifying reasoning: the claim–evidence–warrant link, argument validity, or boundary conditions. Items that only ask for something to be described or present do not qualify.

Scoring: Y = met, P = partly met, N = not met, ? = could not be established from what was read.

## 3. Searches

All searches were run on 2026-10-16.

### 3.2 Bibliographic databases

- Europe PMC REST `search`, resultType=lite, pageSize 30. Round 1 used the default sort, which returned date order in practice; round 2 used `sort=CITED desc`.
- OpenAlex `/works`. Round 1 used `search=` with relevance ranking; round 2 used `title.search` and `abstract.search` filters sorted by `cited_by_count:desc`.
- Crossref `/works?query.bibliographic=` (relevance ranking, no mailto, 4 s pause between calls).
- arXiv API (relevance ranking, 4 s pause).

Crossref and OpenAlex `search` ignore Boolean operators and return very large relevance-ranked sets. ## 5. Conclusion

Across 5 sources (the EQUATOR library and its development registers, Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Crossref and arXiv), with 18 EQUATOR terms, a grep over all 709 library titles, 3 category browses, 119 development registrations and 28 database queries (701 titles screened), **no item met all three criteria cleanly.**

**The closest item is the JBI expert-opinion appraisal checklist (2025).** It is consensus-based (adapted Delphi), it applies to opinion text, and it has an item asking whether the opinion "demonstrate[s] a logically defended argument", pointing to Toulmin. It is a partial counterexample. It meets (c) only in part: it is a reviewer's inclusion tool for systematic reviews, and it asks one overall question rather than checking each step from evidence to claim.

**Effect on S2-2's wording.** The second half of S2-2 holds as worded only because of the qualifier "whether each step from evidence to claim holds". Without that qualifier the JBI tool would arguably falsify it. Accuracy would improve if the manuscript named JBI 2025 as the nearest neighbour, alongside SANRA, VANRA and Nashwan. That is the author's call; the manuscript was not edited.

**Hirschheim 2008** is the closest item aimed at conceptual papers specifically. It fails (a) as one editor's guidance, and its content could not be checked.

The first half of S2-2 ("exists but is scattered") is consistent with what was found: editor and author guidance spread across marketing (Jaakkola, Hulland, Hollebeek), management (Whetten), IS (Hirschheim, Gregor & Hevner), software engineering (Ralph) and health (SANRA, JBI, Nashwan).

## 6. Limitations

- **Databases not searched:** Scopus, Web of Science, PsycINFO, ERIC, PhilPapers, SSRN (searched only indirectly through Crossref and OpenAlex), Google Scholar and law or humanities indexes. Philosophy and argumentation-theory venues are the most likely home of an argument-evaluation standard outside the reporting-guideline tradition, and they were reached only through OpenAlex and Crossref relevance ranking.
- **Screening depth:** only the top 30 per query were screened. Crossref and OpenAlex `search` counts come from loose relevance matching. Europe PMC round 1 came back date-ordered and round 2 citation-ordered, so a recent, rarely cited guideline could fall outside both.
- **EQUATOR scope:** the library is health research; its search appears to match titles only; and the register pages are not exhaustive by EQUATOR's own statement.
- **Language:** English queries only, though a few non-English records appeared (for example a Portuguese-origin CRT-EE).
- **Grey literature:** society position statements, journal author instructions and reviewer forms (for example, journals' own criteria for perspective articles) were not searched. A journal-level checklist would fail (a) but could narrow "scattered".
- **Full text:** Hirschheim 2008, LETTERS, Nashwan 2026 (in this run) and RIGHT were judged from abstracts or registry notes.
- **Single screener:** one agent screened everything, with no second-reviewer check of inclusion decisions.
