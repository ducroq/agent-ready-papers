## Summary
Instructions for writing an "Analytic Summary": "the logical skeleton of a paper", which is explicitly not an abstract, an ordinary summary or an extended summary, and need not be "narratively adequate". It is at most 3 A4 pages, plus at most 1 page of background, with extra pages allowed for an exceptionally long paper.

## Key Findings
- Making one is "a meaningful project iff the paper consists mainly of claims and arguments".
- Contents: the results of any conceptual analyses; the claims the author argues for or against; the arguments for or against each claim, with "a brief indication how the argument goes"; the possible objections the author considers, and the author's responses; few if any quotations.
- Method: read with the summary in mind, and mark "claims, arguments, premises, objections, responses" in the margin.
- "Few authors sadly attain this level of clarity and explicitness", so the reader may have to resort "to conjecturing premises of the author and re-constructing arguments rationally".
- Failing to find the skeleton counts against the paper, hedged: "in case of failure: probably a bad paper". Yet "in every paper, the Reader can and should find some ingredients".

## Relevance to agent-ready-papers
It maps almost one-to-one onto the DR-027 model (`docs/glossary.md`):

| Analytic summary | Framework |
|---|---|
| Results of conceptual analyses | Declared starting points (definitions) |
| Claims argued for or against | Claims |
| Arguments and how they go | Grounds plus warrant |
| Objections and responses | Objection (engaged, recorded with its target); the author's responses have no term of their own |
| Conjecturing premises, rational reconstruction | Hidden warrant or unstated assumption, registered explicitly |
| Scope: papers made mainly of claims and arguments | Paper 1's scope: non-empirical papers |

Two differences:
- It has no qualifier, confidence tier or source check. It reconstructs the argument without rating how strongly each claim may be stated.
- It is the *reader's* reconstruction of someone else's paper, whereas the claim registry is the *author's* own record.

It is evidence that this kind of structured argument reconstruction already exists in university teaching. As unpublished course material, it cannot support a claim in a paper.

## Open Questions
- **An independent check (an idea, not adopted or tested).** Someone other than the author writes an analytic summary of a draft without seeing its registry, and the two are compared. Disagreements would show where the argument is unclear: a claim the reader can't find, a warrant the reader reconstructs differently, or an objection the author never engaged with. This bears on the referees' "no independent application" (Paper 1), on DR-029's `confirmed` and its Open Question about who finds the strongest objection, and on Paper 2's evaluation (DR-006). Unlike the blind re-rating, which rates the author's rows, this reconstructs the argument from scratch.
