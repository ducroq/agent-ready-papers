## Key Findings
- 7 detectors (incl. GPTZero, Originality.AI, ZeroGPT) on 91 TOEFL essays and 88 US 8th-grade essays: "they misclassified over half of the TOEFL essays as 'AI-generated' (average false positive rate: 61.22%)"; near-perfect accuracy on the US essays (Results, p. 2).
- "89 of the 91 TOEFL essays (97.80%) are flagged as AI-generated by at least one detector" (p. 2).
- Enriching the TOEFL essays' vocabulary with ChatGPT cut the average FPR from 61.22% to 11.77%. Simplifying the US essays raised it from 5.19% to 56.65%. The mechanism is perplexity: plainer word choice reads as machine-like.
- Evasion: one self-edit prompt cut detection of generated college essays from 100% to 13% (Fig. 2a).

## Caveats (tier discipline)
- The authors call it a pilot, and the samples are small. Detectors were accessed on 2023-03-15, with GPT-3.5.
- The two essay sets differ in more than native language (genre, prompt, age).
- **Not evidence that current (2026) detectors are biased.** No source in this registry retests non-native text on current tools.
- Tier **B**.

## Relevance
- Adopter-risk note (README Common Questions).
- The mechanism matters: detectors that key on predictability penalise plain prose, whoever writes it.
