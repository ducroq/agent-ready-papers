## Key Findings
- Abstract: "Despite the increasing number of DSR publications there is still no common sense and no comprehensive guidance how to present DSR in scientific literature."
- Introduction: "there is still a need for support in how to publish (and to review) DSR contributions (Goes 2014; Gregor and Hevner 2013)".

## Relevance
Paper 1 S2-2: agrees with Gregor & Hevner 2013 that design science lacks consensus guidance on presenting and judging papers, two years after that paper's own schema.

## Caveats
- Research-in-Progress paper (reviewed, but short and preliminary). It proposes a configurable publication schema itself.
- Counterpoint in the same paper: "There is consensus in the DSR community that Hevner et al.'s (2004) so-called 7 guidelines (GL) explicate DSR specifics precisely". The gap it states is about presenting DSR papers, not about what DSR must do.
- Not independent of Gregor & Hevner 2013: its introduction sentence cites them. Only the abstract sentence is its own finding.
- Information systems only; 2015.
- Tier **A** (peer-reviewed conference; weaker than a journal article).
